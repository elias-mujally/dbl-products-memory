## Visual-disclosure controls and residual risk

Apply `SetWindowDisplayAffinity(WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE=0x11)` to the helper's own top-level window before it can draw a value; qualify its result, supported OS/DWM composition and affinity readback on this device. Failure means STOP before real disclosure, not fallback to ordinary capture. It affects supported capture paths, not physical cameras or arbitrary local inspection, and does not protect Bitwarden's separate window. [Microsoft API scope and limitations](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winuser/nf-winuser-setwindowdisplayaffinity), [affinity readback](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winuser/nf-winuser-getwindowdisplayaffinity).

No secure-desktop claim: this is a normal owner-session window. UI Automation, accessibility hooks, process injection, keyloggers, screen recorders not respecting the API, remote-control software, observers and external cameras remain threats. Nonselectable custom drawing reduces accidental copy and standard control-text exposure, but must not be advertised as preventing a malicious same-user application. Known active capture/remote session means STOP; owner closes such tools manually, without agent policy edits. Never use Codex screenshots/computer-use capture while either application displays a real password. Bitwarden reveal is also visible and cannot inherit the helper's capture protection. Reduce that exposure by preparing the saved item first, revealing only during retrieval, then hiding it immediately. Owner acceptance of prior clipboard risk does not automatically authorize this different visual risk.

Keeping 48 characters avoids introducing a new password-strength/policy design. Manual typing adds burden and transcription risk; exact saved-value verification mitigates a wrong SQL write, not compromise of the visible value. No lower-strength replacement, printable-password file or screen-capture workaround is proposed.
